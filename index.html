Before you click around: what this prototype is, and how to read it

This is a working sketch of the new APL-UW website. You can move through it like a real site, but it's built to test structure and layout, not to be the finished thing. A few minutes here will make your review more useful.

The basics

What is an interactive mockup?

It's a set of linked page designs that behave enough like a website for you to try it out. Menus open, links take you to other pages, and the layout adjusts to your screen. Behind the pages there's no content system, database or search engine. It's a model of the site, not the site itself.

What you can do

  • Use the main menu. Explore research, Work with us, Join us and About each open a menu of where you can go next.
  • Follow links between pages. Cards, "All …" links, breadcrumbs and the footer go where they would on the real site.
  • See every kind of page. Landing pages, listings, projects, people, stories, events, datasets and more: 24 pages covering every page type.
  • Try it on your phone, or narrow your browser window. The menu folds behind a Menu button and the pages rearrange.
  • Judge the structure. Can you find what you came for? Does each page tell you where to go next?

What it can't do yet

  • Search or filter. Search boxes, filters, sorting and page numbers are there to show the layout. They always show the same sample results.
  • Send anything. Forms, share buttons and "add to calendar" don't submit or save.
  • Show every page. Each page type has one example. All five research themes open the polar theme, and every project opens BICEP.
  • Show final content. Photos, much of the writing, names and dates are placeholders. Real text and images come later.
  • Open outside documents. Publications, PDFs, videos and news coverage would open other sites. Here those links go nowhere.
What to expect

Four things you'll see on almost every page

These are deliberate. They keep the conversation on how the site works rather than on wording or photo choices, which come later.

Why it's laid out this way

Built around what people come to do

Much of today's site follows the Lab's own structure, so visitors need to know how APL-UW is organized to find what they need. The new site starts from what someone is trying to do. The main menu has four doors, each answering a question a visitor brings.

What do you study?Explore researchFive research themes, then projects, publications, reports and data.
Can we work together?Work with usSponsored research, facilities and vessels, licensing, data and finding an expert.
Can I work or study here?Join usCareers, student research, the summer program and fellowships, in one place.
Who are you?AboutLeadership, departments, history, news, events and annual reports.

Five themes instead of a long list of areas

Research is grouped into five plain-language themes, like "Sound in the sea." Each theme opens onto the more specific research areas inside it. A newcomer gets a way in, and specialists can still go straight to the full, filterable list.

A story first, then the full list

The research landing page explains what APL-UW works on before offering a listing. Browsing everything is one click away for people who already know what they want.

One site instead of three

The Polar Science Center site and the careers site are folded in. Polar research sits under its theme, and jobs sit with student programs under Join us.

People and departments are still easy to reach

They aren't in the main menu, but every project, story and theme links to the people and departments behind it. Find an expert is one click from Work with us.

Housekeeping moves to the footer

News, events, data, annual reports, support, contact, the campus map and the intranet live in the footer, so the top of every page stays focused on the four main paths.

A familiar look

The design keeps the Lab's ocean blue, gold and UW lockup, with a light, open page body. It aims to be clearer, not trendier.
