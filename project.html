Fish-like vehicle swims in Portage Bay

Bio Inspired Covert Electrostatic Propulsor (BICEP): a future vision for autonomous underwater vehicles

Department
Environmental & Information Systems
Sponsor
Office of Naval Research
Status

Years
[Start – end]

BICEP replaces conventional electric motors with soft, biologically inspired artificial muscles that flap a fish-like tail, a quieter way to move an underwater vehicle.

“We're taking soft robotics out of the lab and into the real world.”

Tom Hansen, Research Scientist/Engineer, Principal

A hydrostatic swimmer

The vehicle's HASEL artificial muscles, from Artimus Robotics, are nearly neutrally buoyant and tolerate high pressure. In acoustic and magnetic tests, they proved hard to detect.

Biomechanics work by NUWC Division Newport's Andrew Tang, including muscular hydrostats and multisegmented vertebrae, shaped the design. The team sees the system as competitive with conventional propulsion, especially for quiet, low-disturbance missions.
